# Records and sensitive data

- Apply the business or legislative rule effective when the action occurred. Preserve earlier decisions and their governing rules when policy changes.
- Treat payment requests, transactions, settlement records, and audit history as records of fact. Prefer append, version, supersede, or explicit correction over destructive mutation.
- Do not weaken authorization, privacy, auditability, accessibility, or historical preservation as a shortcut.
- Surface uncertainty when a requirement may conflict with legislation, an approved business decision, or an established retention rule.
- Treat customer identity, documents, payment evidence, authentication data, and operational logs as sensitive. Use synthetic or redacted data for fixtures and verification. Disclose real data only for a specific authorized need.
- Preserve useful error causes and actionable context without exposing sensitive data in logs, screenshots, captured requests, or responses.
